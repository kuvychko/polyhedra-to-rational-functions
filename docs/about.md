# About

A project by [Igor Kuvychko](https://www.linkedin.com/in/igor-kuvychko). The computations and renders use
[complexplorer](https://github.com/kuvychko/complexplorer), a library for visualizing complex
functions.

## Background and acknowledgments

### Elias Wegert and complex ornaments

This project owes a particular debt to **Elias Wegert**. His book, *Visual Complex Functions: An
Introduction with Phase Portraits* (2012), provides a rich introduction to understanding complex
functions through their visual structure. Phase portraits make zeros, poles, and their
multiplicities visible through the winding of color, offering an intuitive companion to algebraic
expressions.

In private correspondence, Wegert suggested the idea of **complex ornaments** to me. I am grateful
for that suggestion, which helped inspire the physical objects explored here. Turning a complex
function into something that can be printed, held, and examined gives these visualizations another
dimension of accessibility and enjoyment. This personal acknowledgment is distinct from the
published references below.

### Polyhedra and rational functions

The connection between polyhedral geometry and rational functions has deep roots in Felix Klein's
work on the symmetries of the Platonic solids. Vertices, face centers, and edge midpoints determine
special point sets on the Riemann sphere; polynomial forms associated with these sets can be
combined into rational invariants.

Oliver Nash's *On Klein's Icosahedral Solution of the Quintic* provides a modern account of this
construction. It is an important reference for the classical examples underlying this project. The
exploration here asks how related constructions behave when extended to more general convex
polyhedra, using explicit rules for placing zeros and poles and assigning their multiplicities.

### Visualization on the Riemann sphere

Visualizing complex functions through magnitude and phase is an established approach. Alongside
Wegert's book, Valíková and Chalmovianský describe three-dimensional visualization over the Riemann
sphere using a function's absolute value and argument. Sandoval-Romero and Hernández-Garduño
present spherical domain coloring and interactive visualization of Möbius transformations.

These works provide context for the project's colored spherical views and radial reliefs. The
present emphasis is on comparing polyhedron-based encoding recipes and carrying selected results
through to printable objects.

### Scope of the exploration

The project builds on established complex analysis, polyhedral invariant theory, and visualization
methods. Its focus is the explicit implementation and comparison of geometric recipes: what they
preserve, what information they discard, how they behave under deformation, and what their reliefs
look like on screen and in print. No claim of priority is made for the individual recipes or
visualization techniques.
