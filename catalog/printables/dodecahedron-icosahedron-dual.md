**A complex function you can hold.**

This sculpture is a picture of a single mathematical function. A rational function on the Riemann sphere is pinned down by its zeros and poles. Here they come from a polyhedron: zeros sit at the 12 vertices of an icosahedron, and poles sit at the 20 vertices of its dual, the dodecahedron. The surface rises where the function's size grows, so **poles become spikes and zeros become pits**. The result has 20 spikes pointing to the corners of a dodecahedron, and 12 pits facing the corners of an icosahedron. It is the icosahedral twin of the Cube–Octahedron Dual, built the same way.

## The math

- Function: f(z) = V(z)⁵ / H(z)³, built from Klein's classical icosahedral forms: V = z(z¹⁰ − 11z⁵ − 1) for the 12 icosahedron vertices, and H = z²⁰ + 228z¹⁵ + 494z¹⁰ − 228z⁵ + 1 for the 20 dodecahedron vertices. One icosahedron vertex sits at infinity, the north pole of the sphere, which is why V has degree 11 rather than 12.
- Zeros of order 5 at the icosahedron's vertices, one for each edge meeting there. Poles of order 3 at the dodecahedron's vertices, one for each side of the icosahedron's triangular faces. The degree is 60.
- The relief has the full symmetry of the icosahedron, mirrors included: 120 symmetries, the largest symmetry group there is for a polyhedron.
- The radius at each point of the sphere follows |f|. The constant is fixed so that |f| has geometric mean 1 over the sphere, which puts "sea level" midway between the spikes and the pits.

## Files

Two sizes, measured tip to tip: **80 mm** and **130 mm**. Both sizes have been printed: the 130 mm whole model, and the 80 mm hanging ornament.

- **Whole model:** one piece for each size.
- **Cut halves:** the model is cut through its centre along a symmetry plane, so the two halves are identical. Print the half file **twice**. Each half lies flat on its cut face.
- **Hanging ornament:** halves **a** and **b** with a 1.5 mm thread hole through one spike, 9 mm from its tip (9.5 mm on the 130 mm version, to keep at least 1.5 mm of wall around the hole). The spike lies in the cut plane, so the hole runs through both halves and lines up when they are glued. Thread it, tie a loop, and hang it on a tree.

## Assembly

- Every half has a 5.2 mm alignment hole in the middle of its cut face, for an **M5 dowel pin (5.00 mm diameter)**:
  - 80 mm: M5 × 10 pin, 6 mm holes;
  - 130 mm: M5 × 20 pin, 11 mm holes.
- With the settings below, the pin gives a light friction fit. Glue the pin into one half, then glue the halves together; the pin keeps them aligned. If your printer makes holes tighter or looser, ream the hole lightly. You can also glue the halves without a pin.
- Glue: a budget cyanoacrylate (super glue) for plastics works well with PLA. I used LOOCTOT glue for plastic.
- Ornament hole: run a 1.5 mm drill bit through it by hand in a pin vise to clean it up. Monofilament (fishing line) makes a good hanging loop. Keep glue away from the hole while assembling.

## Print settings (tested)

- PLA, 0.4 mm nozzle
- 0.20 mm layers ("QUALITY" profile)
- 15% gyroid infill
- Halves print cut face down. No supports are needed.
