**A complex function you can hold.**

This sculpture is a picture of a single mathematical function. A rational function on the Riemann sphere is pinned down by its zeros and poles. Here they come from a regular icosahedron: poles sit at its 12 vertices, and zeros at the midpoints of its 30 edges. The surface rises where the function's size grows, so **poles become spikes and zeros become pits**. The result is a crown of 12 tall spikes, with a pit in the saddle between every pair of neighbours.

## The math

- Function: f(z) = T(z)² / V(z)⁵, built from Klein's classical icosahedral forms: V = z(z¹⁰ − 11z⁵ − 1) for the 12 vertices, and T = z³⁰ − 522z²⁵ − 10005z²⁰ − 10005z¹⁰ + 522z⁵ + 1 for the 30 edge midpoints. One vertex sits at infinity, the north pole of the sphere, which is why V has degree 11 rather than 12.
- 12 poles of order 5 (one for each edge meeting at a vertex) and 30 zeros of order 2: degree 60.
- The relief has the full symmetry of the icosahedron, mirrors included: 120 symmetries, the largest symmetry group there is for a polyhedron.
- The radius at each point of the sphere follows |f|. The constant is fixed so that |f| has geometric mean 1 over the sphere, which puts "sea level" midway between the spikes and the pits.

## Files

Two sizes, measured tip to tip: **80 mm** and **130 mm**. With 42 features, it shows its detail best at 130 mm.

- **Whole model:** one piece for each size.
- **Cut halves:** the model is cut through its centre along a symmetry plane, so the two halves are identical. Print the half file **twice**. Each half lies flat on its cut face.
- **Hanging ornament:** halves **a** and **b** with a 1.5 mm thread hole through one spike, 9 mm from its tip. No spike lies exactly in the cut plane, so the hole runs through a spike next to it, entirely within one half. Thread it, tie a loop, and hang it on a tree.

## Assembly

- Every half has a 5.2 mm alignment hole in the middle of its cut face, for an **M5 dowel pin (5.00 mm diameter)**:
  - 80 mm: M5 × 10 pin, 6 mm holes;
  - 130 mm: M5 × 20 pin, 11 mm holes.
- With the settings below, the pin gives a light friction fit. Glue the pin into one half, then glue the halves together; the pin keeps them aligned. If your printer makes holes tighter or looser, ream the hole lightly. You can also glue the halves without a pin.
- Glue: a budget cyanoacrylate (super glue) for plastics works well with PLA. I used LOOCTOT glue for plastic.
- Ornament hole: run a 1.5 mm drill bit through it by hand in a pin vise to clean it up. Monofilament (fishing line) makes a good hanging loop. Keep glue away from the hole while assembling.

## Print settings (tested)

- PLA, 0.4 mm nozzle
- 0.20 mm layers ("QUALITY" profile)
- 15% gyroid infill
- Halves print cut face down. No supports are needed.
